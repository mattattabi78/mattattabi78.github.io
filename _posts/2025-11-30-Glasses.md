## Abstraction

Design inspiration often requires extensive reference exploration and iterative trial and error, which can become a bottleneck in the creative process. Although recent large-scale image generation models offer new possibilities for creative support, **general-purpose foundation models often lack the adaptability needed for specialized design domains** such as eyewear. Furthermore, mainstream generative interfaces tend to position image generation as a passive consumptive experience rather than an active design process.

To address these limitations, this project proposes an AI pipeline that supports designers by presenting unexpected formal fragments generated through interactions among multiple visual concepts in latent space **without any training**. Ultimately, the project aims to reduce the time spent on inspiration discovery while enabling designers to identify and develop original design directions.

## Objectives

The objectives of this project can be summarized into two main goals: **the generation of valid design outcomes.**

### Generation of Valid Design Outcomes

According to Kees Dorst Visser (2006), designing can be understood as a process of constructing representations, in which designers generate, transform, and progressively refine various external and internal representations. This process requires **multiple levels of abstraction**, through which diverse visual concepts are selectively interpreted, filtered, and integrated within the designer’s cognition. The resulting synthesis ultimately leads to design outcomes.

Through this process, design outputs acquire persuasive value within the consumer market and are able to deliver a more advanced design experience to users as *valid designs*. In this sense, a valid design can be understood as the result of a cognitive transfer process in which the formal and perceptual structures, constitute an image are abstracted into conceptual representations and then recombined under new contexts and constraints. In this project, this sequence of operations is defined as the designer’s **process of abstraction**.

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image1.png" class="img-medium" alt="image1">
**Figure 1**. Comparison of fusion results from two input images. General-purpose generative models often produce overly input-dependent and one-dimensional outputs, with limited evidence of abstraction.

From this perspective, existing large-scale generative models(ChatGPT 5.2, Nano banana pro) show limited adaptability to specialized design tasks. Most B2C generative models are optimized to produce broadly acceptable image outputs across diverse domains, which often makes them less effective for niche applications such as eyewear design. Moreover, techniques for preserving consistency across sequential image generation may inadvertently increase dependence on the input image, thereby constraining output diversity.

As a result, the general-purpose architecture of large-scale generative models—despite its versatility and consistency—can **paradoxically inhibit deeper forms of design abstraction**. This project therefore investigates training-free methods for improving model adaptability, with the goal of enabling the model to reproduce the **abstraction process** that underlies the generation of valid design outcomes.

## Methods

### Improved Generative Workflow through IP-Adapter and ControlNet

Glass Lab is built on IP-Adapter and ControlNet to adapt the Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) model to the specialized domain of eyewear design without additional training. Users can provide one composition image and one or more reference images as inputs to the model.

The composition image serves as the source of low-level structural information, such as the overall composition and global form of the generated output. In contrast, the reference images function as visual references to be injected into the generation process, providing higher-level information such as material qualities, color, and localized formal characteristics.

The composition image and the reference images are processed through ControlNet and IP-Adapter, respectively, and are injected into the image generation process of the SDXL model. As a result, the generated image preserves the overall structure of the composition image while naturally incorporating the design “concepts” derived from the reference images.

**Figure 2.** Simplified architecture of Glass Lab. Given one composition image and one or more reference images, the system uses IP-Adapter to abstract visual concepts from the references and naturally incorporate them into the generated output.

### IP-Adapter

IP-Adapter is an adapter-based image encoder introduced by Tencent AI Lab in 2023 to inject image-based conditions into text-prompt-centered diffusion models in a lightweight manner. During the image encoding process, the input image is progressively compressed into conceptual and semantic representations. These compressed representations are then expanded and materialized into diverse visual forms during the image generation process. This mechanism of compression and expansion is structurally analogous to what this project defines as the designer’s **abstraction process**, in which a designer experiences an object and extracts conceptual meaning from it.

In the generation pipeline, the reference image is embedded into the model’s each cross-attention block through IP-Adapter’s pretrained CLIP image encoder as a token. The resulting high-dimensional conceptual representation functions as a dominant design direction throughout the generation process, guiding the model to produce and transform a range of visual details in relation to that direction. As a result, we employ the adapter-based encoder structure of IP-Adapter as a simple yet effective mechanism for generating what we define as **valid design** outcomes.

In addition, IP-Adapter’s embedding process is used not only for extracting concepts from reference images, but also for helping the model **internalize the concept of eyewear itself**. A curated set of eyewear images with diverse designs is embedded through IP-Adapter and introduced to the model as a shared conceptual representation of the domain.

This compressed representation captures both common structural characteristics and stylistic diversity across eyewear designs. As a result, the generated outputs remain conceptually aligned with the eyewear domain while still allowing the reference images to blend coherently within the model’s latent space.

At the implementation level, both the reference images and the curated design images are incorporated into the model’s cross-attention blocks as weighted combinations. When multiple reference images are used, their weights can be independently adjusted to control the degree of influence of each reference.

### ControlNet

Whereas IP-Adapter conveys conceptual information about eyewear to the model, ControlNet plays the complementary role of strictly constraining visual form, thereby maintaining a consistent structural framework for the generated design. Introduced in 2023 by researchers at Stanford University, ControlNet is a network extension method that preserves the weights of a pretrained model while adding a condition-specific branch, enabling the generation process to reliably follow structural inputs such as edges, depth, or pose.

In conjunction with IP-Adapter, ControlNet constrains the visual boundaries of the eyewear design and ensures that these structures are consistently maintained throughout image generation. ControlNet is primarily attached to the down and middle blocks of the UNet since structural conditions such as edges, pose, and depth are most effectively imposed during the early and intermediate stages of feature formation. The overall form of the composition image is extracted using the Canny edge detection algorithm, and the resulting edge map is provided to ControlNet to guide the structural composition of the generated output.

The degree of structural control can be continuously adjusted through the **ControlNet scale**, allowing users to specify how strictly the generated output should follow the composition image.

By integrating IP-Adapter and ControlNet, Glass Lab adapts SDXL to the eyewear domain without additional training while preserving the expressive richness of the base model. In addition, the system enhances controllability by allowing users to dynamically adjust the influence of both networks during generation.

## Experiment

This project was exhibited over a four-day period from November 20 to November 23, 2025, during the 2025 Art & Technology Conference (ATC) at the Fabrication Lab in Building X of Sogang university (Seoul, Korea). The exhibition was presented as a participatory generative AI project in which visitors could directly intervene in the eyewear design generation process and observe the resulting outputs.

During the exhibition, a **total of 129 eyewear images were generated**, reflecting the accumulation of diverse inputs and repeated attempts by a broad public audience. Visitors selected input images based on their own interests and visual preferences, including sculptures, everyday objects, and character imagery. In addition to using pre-prepared images, they were also able to explore references through web search and determine the direction of the design themselves. As a result, the project functioned as an interactive design system in which audience participation shaped the process from inspiration selection to final output.

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image4.png" class="img-medium" alt="image4">

**Figure 10**. Exhibition input images (top) and corresponding generated eyewear outputs (bottom).

When users selected sculptural objects as visual references (**Figure 10**), the system generated eyewear designs by translating the formal characteristics and visual impressions of the source objects into wearable forms. Curvilinear silhouettes and twisted structures were reflected in the frame contours and temple geometry, while material impressions such as glass or metal informed the surface treatment of the lenses and frame.

Color palettes and decorative elements also influenced the generated results. Repetitive patterns and ornamental motifs were reinterpreted as frame details, and the overall balance and visual weight of the source objects tended to be reflected in the proportions and structural composition of the eyewear. Rather than reproducing the source object directly, the system abstracted its formal properties and reconstructed them within the functional constraints of eyewear design.

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image5.png" class="img-medium" alt="image5">

**Figure 11**. Exhibition input images (top) and corresponding generated eyewear outputs (bottom).

When users selected objects with distinctive color palettes or unusual material qualities as references (**Figure 11**), the system actively incorporated these attributes into the generated eyewear designs. Soft or wrinkled surfaces, translucent or glossy materials, and even unrealistic color combinations influenced the texture, thickness, and color treatment of the frames.

Notably, even when the relationship between the input image and eyewear was not intuitively predictable, the system was still able to generate plausible eyewear forms. This suggests that the AI did not simply replicate visible features, but instead interpreted the material impressions and atmospheric qualities of the source images and reconstructed them into a new design object. In particular, qualities such as softness, rigidity, artificiality, and organicity tended to be translated into the structural and formal emphasis of the eyewear frame.

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image6.png" class="img-medium" alt="image6">

**Figure 12**. Exhibition input images (top) and corresponding generated eyewear outputs (bottom).

When users selected images with a strong character identity as input (Figure 12), the system did not directly reproduce the character itself. Instead, it selectively extracted core visual features and translated them into the eyewear design. Symbolic colors, repetitive forms, decorative motifs, and overall atmosphere were often retained, while directly representational features such as faces or bodies were omitted. This suggests that the AI interpreted character identity not as an image to be copied, but as a set of design languages to be reconfigured.

The way in which character identity was reflected also varied depending on where that identity was visually located. When identity was primarily associated with color or symbolic motifs, these elements tended to be incorporated into the frame and its details while preserving the overall atmosphere of the character. In contrast, when identity was embedded in repetitive patterns, textures, or object-like visual components, it was more often expressed through surface treatment and structural form. This indicates that the AI did not treat character identity as a uniform category, but rather applied it flexibly according to the nature of the identity-bearing visual elements.

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image7.png" class="img-medium" alt="image7">

**Figure 13**. Exhibition input images (top) and corresponding generated eyewear outputs (bottom).

When users selected everyday objects as inspiration images (Figure 13), the system generated eyewear designs by interpreting the formal structure and functional impression of the source objects. Rather than replicating their external appearance, the AI extracted structural logic and visual characteristics and reconstructed them into forms suitable for eyewear.

Sharp silhouettes and strong directional qualities tended to produce frames with angular contours or streamlined flows, whereas repetitive structures and mechanical elements were more often translated into structural detailing around the lenses and temples. When these properties coexisted within a single input, the resulting designs tended to combine angular and streamlined formal qualities. This suggests that the AI interpreted not only visible form, but also the perceived usability and functional attributes of the source object.

More importantly, **the system did not function as a uniform generator or as a substitute for the user’s creative role**. Instead, it operated as an interpretive tool that visually responded to the direction established by the audience. By selectively translating form, color, material, and identity-related features according to the nature of the input, the system enabled participants to perceive how their choices shaped the resulting design. This demonstrates a collaborative creative structure in which human judgment and AI generation operated in tandem.

## Conclusion, Limitation and Expected Impact

This project proposed Glasses Generator, an eyewear-specific generative pipeline designed as a supportive tool that simulates the designer’s **abstraction process**. The project defined valid design generation as a process of cognitive transfer in which formal and perceptual structures are abstracted, translated into conceptual representations, and recombined under new contexts and constraints. In this sense, it is not merely a system for producing better images. Rather, its significance lies in supporting a designer-driven process of rapid exploration, selective convergence, enabling users to discover multiple promising design directions and progressively shape them into persuasive outcomes. 

To strengthen Glasses Generator as a practical design tool, future work should focus on two directions: establishing a systematic evaluation framework for **valid design outcomes**, and refining the system through lightweight tuning or the incorporation of individual and brand-specific styles. In particular, integrating structural rules and part-level concepts for key eyewear components may improve the plausibility and design resolution of generated outputs.

More broadly, Glasses Generator demonstrates **the potential of generative AI as a collaborative design tool rather than a replacement for the designer**. By reducing the burden of inspiration search and iterative exploration, the system can support both efficiency and creativity in early-stage design. Beyond eyewear, the proposed combination of **abstraction-based image conditioning and structural control** may also be extended to other industrial design domains where formal experimentation must coexist with functional and structural constraints.

## Service Description

### Provision of a Professional Generative Design Experience via Website

The adoption of large-scale generative models in professional eyewear design remains limited. This is due not only to the difficulty of generating valid design outcomes, but also to the discontinuous and overly simplified interaction paradigms through which designers engage with these models. Such interfaces are not well suited to the demands of specialized design workflows.

We highlight the limitations of the graphical user interfaces (GUIs) of existing commercial generative models and proposes a custom web-based platform, **Glasses Generator**, to provide a more intuitive and specialized design generation experience. By prioritizing the presentation of a large number of design alternatives, the platform is intended to support creative exploration without requiring complex prompt engineering or technical expertise. Ultimately, this research proposes an integrated web-based environment for eyewear design generation.

This project developed a GUI that externalizes the internal control process of the AI pipeline, enabling users to engage with design generation without requiring technical expertise. The system provides default generation parameters based on internal optimization experiments, while still allowing users to modify them as needed. This design aims to balance ease of use with user controllability and creative flexibility.

### Intuitive Selection through a Radial Layout

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image8.png" class="img-medium" alt="image8">

A radial layout was used to facilitate comparison among the six generated outputs. This interface enables users to quickly identify subtle differences across alternatives and make intuitive selections. The key interaction principle of this process is **convergence**. Each user-selected output serves as the basis for the next generation step, allowing the design to become progressively more specific through iterative selection. As a result, the process begins with broad exploration and gradually converges toward a form that better reflects the user’s intended design direction.

### **Intuitive Local Refinement through a Brush-Based Editing Tool**

<img src="/assets/images/Glasses Generator/image9.png" class="img-medium" alt="image9">

To allow selected designs to be refined according to user intent, a canvas-based inpainting function was implemented. Rather than regenerating the entire image, users can intuitively modify only the regions they wish to change by painting over them with a brush tool. By regenerating only the selected area while taking the surrounding visual context into account, allowing the edited region to blend naturally with the rest of the design. Through this iterative refinement process, even users without professional expertise can easily adjust details and develop the design toward their intended outcome.
