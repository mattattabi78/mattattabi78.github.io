## Instruction

MusicGen is comprised of a single-stage transformer LM together with efficient token interleaving patterns. MusicGen consist in an autoregressive transformer based decoder, conditioned on a text or melody representation. Authors introduce a novel modeling framework, which generalizes to various codebook interleaving patterns.

## About Audio Tokenization

They used EnCodec, a convolutional auto-encoder with a latent space quantized using Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) with adversarial reconstruction loss. EnCodec is neural based audio codec which convert audio to discrete token or vice versa. Given reference audio random variable $$X \in \mathbb R^{d \cdot f_s}$$ with $$d$$ to audio duration and $$f_s$$ as sampling rate. This representation is then quantized into $$Q \in \{ 1, \dots, M\}^{d \cdot f_r \times K}$$ with $$K$$ is number of codebooks and $$M$$ is codebook’s size.

**Codebook** is dictionary of convert continuous audio signal to discrete tokens. For time $$t$$ in continuous audio wave, we can denote the latent vector $$z$$ in $$\mathbb R^d$$. To treat it with transformer, the $$z$$ is replaced with most similar token which means the index of codebook.

As the continuous audio form is too heavy(1 sec of 32kHz needs 32,000 samples), codebook is essential to treat audio wave with transformer based model.

MusicGen has Residual Vector Quantization (**RVQ**), the method that use multiple codebook for rich expression of generated wave.

<img src="/assets/images/MusicGen/image1.png" class="img-medium" alt="image2">

In this figure, each time step is composed of 4 quantized values($$k_1$$ to $$k_4$$). When doing autoregressive modeling, we can flatten or interleave them in various ways.

But these codebook did not contain semantic intentionally because Encodec was intended to reconstructing audio rather than target understand semantic of audio. Thus, it has clear limitation which has week semantic disentanglement.

An autoregressive model requires a discrete random sequence $$U \in \{1, …, M \}^S$$ with $$S$$ the sequence length. We can then model the distribution with $$U_0 = 0$$ as a begining token of sequence.

$$
\begin{equation}
\forall t > 0, \ \  p_t (U_{t-1}, \dots , U_0) = \mathbb{P}[U_t \mid U_{t-1}, \dots , U_0]
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
$$

And the second sequence of a random variables $$\tilde{U}$$ using the auto-regressive density $p$ as..

$$
\begin{equation}
\forall t > 0, \ \  \mathbb P \Big[ \tilde{U}_t \mid \tilde{U}_{t-1}, … , \tilde{U}_0 \Big] = p_t \Big( \tilde U_{t-1}, … , \tilde U_0 \Big)
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
$$

which means that if we can fit perfect estimate $$\hat{p}$$ of $$p$$ with a deep learning model, then we can fit the distribution of $$U$$.

Know, quantized representation $$Q$$ with number of $$K$$ codebooks, they flatten out $$Q$$ $$(S = d \cdot f_r \cdot K)$$ (where $$d \cdot f_r$$ means number of time frame). And using (1) and (2), they could theoretically fit an model of the distribution of $$Q$$ with autoregressive process. But this flatten way increased complexity with part of the gain coming from the lowest sample rate $$f_r$$ being lost.

### Inexact Autoregressive Decomposition

To solve this problem, they put codebooks to predict in parallel under the ignore dependency between codebooks. 

Let the $$V$$ as copied sequence of $$Q$$ ($$V_{t,k} = Q_{t,k}$$). $$V_t$$ means the concatenation of all codebooks at time $$t$$.

$$
\begin{equation} p_{t, k} = (V_{t-1}, \dots, V_0) = \mathbb P \big[ V_{t, k}  \mid V_{t-1}, \cdot, \dots,  V_0 \big] \tag{3} \end{equation}
$$

And let’s define recursively,

$$
\begin{equation}
\forall t > 0, \ \ \forall k,  \ \ \mathbb P \Big[ \tilde{V}_{t, k}  \Big] = p_{t,k} \Big( \tilde V_{t-1}, … , \tilde V_0 \Big)
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
$$

Unlike in (2), it can not guarantee $$\tilde{V}$$ follows the same distribution as $$V$$ anymore. Such a decomposition in inexact way, but allows to keep the original frame rate which can considerably speed up training and inference. And empirically, code book parallel prediction is quite stable because they share previous informations ($$V_{t-1}, \dots, V_0$$). And they experiment with various decompositions and measured impact of each inexact decomposition which named codebook interleaving patterns.

## Model Architecture

**Codebook projection and positional embedding**

Only some codebooks are present at each pattern step $$P_s$$. As each codebook is present at most once or not at all, if it is present, they use embedding table with $$N$$ entries and dimension $$D$$ to represent the associated value from $$Q$$. Then they sum the contribution from each codebook after this transformation.

**Transformer Decoder**

The input is fed into a transformer with $$L$$ layer and a dimension $$D$$. Each layer consists of a causal self-attention block. And then a cross-attention blcok that is fed with the conditioning signal $$C$$ (tensor of condition wave). The layer ends with a fully connected block consisting of a linear layer from $$D$$ to $$4 *D$$ channels, a $$ReLU$$, and a linear layer block to $$D$$ channels. The attention and fully connected blocks are wrapped with a residual skip connection. And layer normalization is applied to each block before being summed with the residual skip connection

### Models and Hyperparameters

They use a non-causal five layer EnCodec model for 32kHz monophonic audio with a stride of 640, resulting in a frame rate of 50 Hz, initial hidden size of 64(doubling at each five layers). The embedding are quantized with a RVQ with four quantizers each with a codebook size of 2048. They trained model on one-second audio segment cropped at random in the audio sequence. They also train autoregressive transformer models at each size of 300M, 1.5B, 3.3B parameters with using memory efficient Flash attention from the xFormers package.
