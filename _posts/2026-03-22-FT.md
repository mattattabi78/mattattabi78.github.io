# Fourier Transformation (푸리에 변환)

“The Fourier Transform (FT) is an integral transform that takes a function as input, and outputs another function that describes the extent to which various frequencies are present in the original function.” - WKIKPEDIA

푸리에 변환은 복잡한 파형을 갖는 하나의 신호 함수를 단순한 신호를 갖는 여러 개의 함수로 변환하는 알고리즘이다. 사실 푸리에 변환을 깊게 이해하기 위해선 각을 복소 공간으로 변환하는 오일러 공식부터 알아야하기 때문에 깊이가 조금 얕을 것 같다. 따라서 이번 포스트에서는 푸리에 변환의 정의와 원리는 간단히 알아보고 특히 이미지 공간에서의 쓰임에 대해 집중적으로 알아보고자 한다.

## Definition

The Fourier transform of a complex-valued function $$f(x)$$ on the real line, the complex valued function $$\hat f(\xi)$$, defined by below.. 

$$
\hat f(\xi) = \int^{\infty}_{-\infty} f(x)e^{-i2\pi \xi x} dx,  \ \ \ \ \ \forall\xi \in \mathbb R
$$

- $$f(x)$$: target function(e.g. signal, image)
- $$\xi$$: target frequency (normally, $$\xi$$Hz)
- $$e^{-i2\pi \xi x}$$: complex exponential sine wave; wave which has frequency $$\xi$$
- $$\int$$: Add up the product of the signal an a sinusoidal wave over all $$x$$

<img src="/assets/images/FT/image1.png" class="img-medium" alt="Figure 1">

This definition directly means sum of multiple between original signal and target frequency $$\xi$$. This can be interpreted as computing similarity (inner product value) between two signal (complex $$f$$ and simple $$\xi$$). Thus, the $$\hat f(\xi)$$ means coefficient of $$\xi$$ in $$f(x)$$.

And the integral over all $$x$$ means the transform measures the global presence of frequency $$\xi$$, **not when it occurs**. 

따라서 타겟 신호와 타겟 주기(주파수)를 푸리에 변환의 입력값으로 넣으면 해당 주기가 갖는 계수(세기)를 반환한다.

### Orthogonality of $$\xi$$

Fourier transform works because complex exponentials of different frequencies are orthogonal.

Let the inner product between two different frequency $$\xi_1$$ and $$\xi_2$$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle e^{i2\pi \xi_1 x},\; e^{i2\pi \xi_2 x} \rangle
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i2\pi \xi_1 x} \cdot e^{-i2\pi \xi_2 x} \, dx \\
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i2\pi (\xi_1 - \xi_2)x} \, dx \\
&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{i2\pi \alpha x} \, dx =0 
\end{aligned}
$$

where $$(\alpha = \xi_1 - \xi_2 \neq 0)$$

This property allows each frequency component to be separated accurately without interference from others. 

Orthogonality between two frequencies amplify the **frequency can be independent axis of wave** coordinate (the energy information of frequencies are never entangle!)

Thus, the energy of combination of multiple signal is simply decomposed with sum of sines wave.

$$
∥f ∥^2= ∥a \xi_1 + b \xi_2∥^2 = \mid a \mid^2 ∥ \xi_1 ∥^2 + ∣b∣^2 ∥ \xi_2 ∥^2
$$

This orthogonal property of waves guarantees us decomposing signals or eliminating noise in mathematical way.

## Fourier transform in computer vision

‘신호’를 다룬다는 관점에서 푸리에 변환은 이미지 도메인까지 확장될 수 있다. 이미지를 픽셀의 집합이 아니라, 주파수 패턴의 조합으로 고려했을 때, 꽤 많은 일들을 할 수 있다.

**What is Spatial frequency?**

Spatial frequency mean how quickly something changes across window space(in an image). Low frequency means the pixel values change slowly while high frequency correspond with pixel values change rapidly. 

<img src="/assets/images/FT/image2.png" class="img-medium" alt="Figure 2">

In computer vision, Fourier Transform decomposes an image into its sines and cosines components. Now, let see how Fourier Transformation in 2D images.

**Definition**

In image, the wave $\xi$ are corresponding to pixel vale $$f(x, y)$$ in point $$(x, y)$$. Since the image is in two-dimension, the Fourier transform is defined like below,

$$
F(u, v) = \int ^{\infty}_{-\infty} \int ^{\infty}_{-\infty} f(x, y) e^{i 2 \pi (ux+vy)}dxdy
$$

where $$u$$ and $$v$$ each means frequency of direction $$x$$ and $$y$$. Thus, $$F(u, v)$$ is the point of how quickly changing.

In this equation, $$ux + vy$$ can be interpreted as inner product $$(u, v) \cdot (x, y)$$ which means the target wave’s phase is determined by projected value for direction $$(u, v)$$.

즉 이미지 푸리에 변환에서 구하고자 하는 파동의 위상은 단순히 $$(x, y)$$ 좌표로 정해지는게 아니라 타겟 방향 $$(u, v)$$로 투영된 좌표에 따라 정해진다. 이 기저는 사실상 “$$(u, v)$$ 방향으로 반복되는 패턴”을 의미하게 된다.

Let see the example with the output of FFT of cat image.

<img src="/assets/images/FT/image3.png" class="img-medium" alt="Figure 3">

This picture shows the cat image frequency in example line and its one-dimension magnitudes per frequencies. In this picture, displacement of each $$x$$ and $$y$$ line in cat image, which can be decomposed with fourier transformation. Therefore, $$F(u, v)$$ means magnitude of two-dimension wave $$(u, v)$$ in intensity $$f(x, y)$$ which is blue and red line in cat image). As we can see, the most of nature image has high magnitude of low frequency (e.g. object existence) and low magnitude of high frequency (e.g. edge of image, texture).

The $$F(u, v)$$ also can be interpreted as inner product $$<f(x, y), e^{i 2 \pi (ux+vy)}>$$ which mean $$F(u, v)$$ is similarity of original signal $$f(x, y)$$ and target basic wave $$e^{i 2 \pi (ux+vy)}$$. Since the inner product is a linear operation, $$F(u, v)$$ can also be viewed as a **linear transformation** that convert spatial basis to frequency basis.

## Usage

### A Fourier Space Perspective on Diffusion model


The property of diffusion model’s forward process results in high-frequency components being corrupted faster and earlier in terms of their Signal-to-noise Ratio (SNR) than low-frequency ones. And the reverse process then generate low-frequency information before high frequency details. In this paper, Authors study the inductive bias of the forward process of diffusion models in Fourier space.

The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is defined like below.

$$
\mathrm{SNR}(f) = \frac{\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{signal}]}{\mathrm{Var}[\mathrm{noise}]}
$$

Low-frequency components have orders of magnitudes hight variances and magnitudes than high-frequency components in natural images.

Their core contributions are…

- The forward process results in a non-uniform rate of noising across frequencies: specifically, high-frequency components are noised faster and earlier.
- Theoretically explain and empirically demonstrate that faster noising as measured by SNR of high-frequency components violated the Gaussian assumption in the backward process which leading to worse generation quality for these frequencies.
    
<img src="/assets/images/FT/image8.png" class="img-medium" alt="Figure 8">

comparing the SNR for DDPM and the alternate EqualSNR in Fourier space.

<img src="/assets/images/FT/image9.png" class="img-medium" alt="Figure 9">

They visualize original DDPM noised high-frequency faster and generate it later  while Equal SNR treat whole frequency equally.

Finally they show that a diffusion model with a Equal SNR forward process alleviates the differential SNR rate by frequency, and performing on par with the standard DDPM forward process in terms of FID.
